Higher Recap — Entity-Occurrence Diagrams

An entity-occurrence (EO) diagram proves the nature of a relationship using real example data. It lists a few actual occurrences of each entity (usually just their primary key values) and draws a line from each occurrence to the occurrences it links to. At Higher, you read the pattern of lines to confirm cardinality:

  • one line each side ↔ one-to-one;
  • fanning out on one side only → one-to-many;
  • criss-crossing in both directions → many-to-many (which must be resolved with a link entity before implementation).

Key Terms

Occurrence
A single instance of an entity — one row of the eventual table, usually written as its primary key value.
Association
A line joining an occurrence in one entity to a related occurrence in another.
Cardinality
Read from the fan pattern: which side's occurrences link to many on the other side?
Participation
Read from unlinked occurrences: an occurrence with no lines means participation at that end is optional.

The New Skill — Identifying Participation from an EO Diagram

Advanced Higher gives the EO diagram a second job: identifying relationship participation. The trick is beautifully simple:

🔑
Look for occurrences with no lines. If even one occurrence of an entity has no association, that entity's participation in the relationship is optional — occurrences can clearly exist without a partner. If every occurrence is linked (and logically must be), participation is mandatory.
Example — the travel agency, read from its EO diagram

Consider an EO diagram for Resort — Hotel — Booking — Customer with sample occurrences. Reading each relationship:

  • Resort : Hotel — every hotel occurrence has exactly one line back to a resort (Resort mandatory — every hotel must be located in a resort), but resort 406 has no hotels linked to it (Hotel optional — a resort may or may not have a hotel). Cardinality: 1:M.
  • Hotel : Booking — every booking links to exactly one hotel (Hotel mandatory), but hotel GL13 has no bookings (Booking optional). Cardinality: 1:M.
  • Customer : Booking — every booking links to exactly one customer (Customer mandatory), but customers 428 and 457 have no bookings — they're on the mailing list and have never booked (Booking optional). Cardinality: 1:M.

💡
Exam wording matters: answer participation questions with evidence from the diagram — "Customer participation is mandatory because every booking occurrence is linked to a customer; Booking participation is optional because customer 428 has no bookings." Point at the data.

Reading an EO Diagram — 2 marks

An EO diagram for a music school shows Tutor and Lesson occurrences:

  • Tut1 — Les1, Les3
  • Tut2 — Les2
  • Tut3 — (no lessons)
  • Every lesson is linked to exactly one tutor.

State, with justification from the diagram, the participation of each entity in the relationship. (2 marks)

Marking Instructions
  • Tutor — optional: Tut3 has no linked lessons, so a tutor can exist without lessons. (1 mark)
  • Lesson — (Tutor's end) mandatory: every lesson occurrence is linked to exactly one tutor, so a lesson cannot exist without a tutor. (1 mark)
